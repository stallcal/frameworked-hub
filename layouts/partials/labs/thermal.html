The relationship

Heating increases the upper-state population. A larger gap suppresses it. Their balance is set by the dimensionless ratio ΔE/RT.

The model, explained.

{{ partial "equation.html" `\frac{N_1}{N_0} = e^{-\Delta E/(RT)}` }}{{ partial "equation.html" `p_1 = \frac{e^{-\Delta E/(RT)}}{1+e^{-\Delta E/(RT)}}` }}

ΔE is the molar energy gap, T is temperature in kelvin, and R is the molar gas constant. The two probabilities sum to one. Each level contains one state, so their degeneracies are equal.

This model describes an ensemble at thermal equilibrium with exactly two available states. It does not show transition rates or a reaction barrier. At high temperature the populations approach 50:50; heating alone does not invert them. Real systems can have additional states and unequal degeneracies.
