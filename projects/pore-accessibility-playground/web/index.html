A precise model with deliberate limits

A rigid circular probe moves among fixed rectangular obstacles. A location must have enough clearance for the whole probe. We then ask which fitting locations connect to the left inlet.

The calculation samples a grid and uses four-neighbor connectivity. To avoid paths that clip obstacle corners, it admits only cells whose centers have clearance greater than the probe radius plus half the cell diagonal. Narrow real paths in this idealized geometry may therefore remain unresolved on a coarse grid. A missing route is not proof that no continuous route exists; compare resolutions near a bottleneck.

The percentages describe conservative, grid-estimated probe-center area in this 2D model. They are not a material’s pore volume, surface area, adsorption capacity, or diffusion rate. Molecular shape, attractions, flexibility, and thermal motion are absent. A probe may fit in a closed pocket without being able to enter it.

Try the wide channel near radius 10, the narrow doorway near radius 5, and the isolated pocket with a small probe. These are illustrative geometries, not models of named MOFs.
