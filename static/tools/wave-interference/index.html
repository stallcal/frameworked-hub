How does the phase difference between two waves affect interference?

Our model uses two coherent waves with the same wavelength and frequency. Their amplitudes add at every position. When the waves have equal amplitudes, a 0° phase difference doubles the amplitude of their sum; a 180° difference makes them cancel completely.

When the amplitudes differ, the result is R = √(A₁² + A₂² + 2A₁A₂ cos φ), where φ is the phase difference. At 180°, the resultant amplitude is |A₁ − A₂|, so unequal waves cannot cancel completely.

A phase difference can also arise when one wave travels farther than the other. The equivalent extra path ΔL is related to phase by φ = 360° × ΔL/λ (modulo 360°). One extra wavelength returns the waves to the same phase; half a wavelength places them 180° out of phase.

The graph is a snapshot of ideal sinusoidal wave amplitudes. It does not show wave intensity, quantum probability density, or how the waves change over time.
