02 / UNDERSTAND THE METHOD

Why width
matters.

A diffraction peak tells us more than where a crystal scatters X-rays. Its shape contains information about the material's microstructure.

01 / THE PRINCIPLE

Sharper peaks, larger domains

When coherent scattering extends across more lattice planes, the size-related contribution to diffraction peak width decreases.

02 / THE MEASUREMENT

FWHM, not half-width

The full width at half maximum is measured across the peak at half its height above an estimated local background.

03 / THE INTERPRETATION

Domain size ≠ particle size

The calculation estimates coherent scattering lengths along a diffraction direction. A particle can contain several distinct coherent domains.

i

A useful estimate, not an automatic ground truth.

Measured width also contains instrument response, lattice strain, defects, and potentially overlapping peaks. For anisotropic frameworks, different reflections may produce different domain-size estimates. Report the assumptions and review fits; consider whole-pattern methods for rigorous microstructural analysis.

03 / A LITTLE HISTORY

A century of
looking closer.

From the first powder diffraction experiments to today's framework materials, the story of the Scherrer equation is a story of extracting structure from scattered light.

1916

Powder X-ray diffraction emerges

Peter Debye and Paul Scherrer describe diffraction from randomly oriented crystallites, providing the basis of the powder method.

1918

Paul Scherrer's size relationship

Scherrer relates diffraction-line broadening to the finite size of coherent crystalline domains.

TODAY

Understanding framework materials

PXRD supports characterization of MOFs and many other crystalline materials, while careful profile analysis remains essential for credible size estimates.
