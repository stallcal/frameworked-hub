How does Bragg's law work?

X-rays scatter from a periodic crystal structure. For certain directions, the path difference between contributions from successive lattice planes equals an integer number of wavelengths, so those contributions reinforce one another.

At fixed wavelength and diffraction order, smaller plane spacing d gives a larger scattering angle 2θ. Conversely, a reflection moving to higher 2θ corresponds to a smaller d-spacing when the wavelength and reflection indexing remain unchanged.

θ is the Bragg angle relative to the lattice planes; 2θ is the angle measured between incident and diffracted beams. The selected (10) and (11) families are an idealized two-dimensional square-lattice example, not a specific crystal structure.

WHY SOME DIRECTIONS REINFORCE AND OTHERS DO NOT

What happens away from the Bragg angle?

Scattered waves are still present away from the Bragg condition, but they generally arrive with different phases. Compare two equal-amplitude contributions from neighboring planes: they add when their extra path is a whole number of wavelengths, and cancel when that path difference is a half-integer number of wavelengths.

Scientific scope: this is a two-wave phase illustration, not a simulated diffraction pattern. Real crystalline scattering depends on many planes, finite-size broadening, atomic structure factors, and the instrument. IUCr explanation of phase and Bragg diffraction.

Model assumptions, limitations & sources

What the models represent: the guided lattice is an ideal 2D square lattice, with d(10) = a and d(11) = a/√2. Dots are lattice points, not atoms or a specific MOF. The wave comparison adds only two equal-amplitude contributions.

What the models cannot determine: experimental peak intensities, phase identity, atomic structure factors, systematic absences, full diffraction patterns, or experimental uncertainty. The scan gives only an idealized normalized finite-stack interference factor. Real diffraction arises from scattering and interference, not literal mirror reflections from atomic planes.

Measurements: wavelength presets are rounded values; detector calibration, instrumental effects and uncertainty propagation are not included. Here q = 4πsinθ/λ = 2πn/d, with q in Å⁻¹.
