Why does the angle change?

The lattice spacing determines the plane spacing, which sets the Bragg angle.

What this model means

Guided lattice view: the (10) and (11) families are drawn in a two-dimensional square lattice with parameter a. Their separations are d(10) = a and d(11) = a / √2. The illustrated dots are translational lattice points, not individual atoms or a real MOF structure.

θ is the angle between a ray and a lattice plane. A powder pattern normally reports 2θ. Here q = 4π sin θ / λ = 2πn / d. Wavelength and spacing are in angstroms; q is in inverse angstroms.

This predicts a geometric diffraction condition, not reflection intensity. Atomic positions, scattering factors, and symmetry determine intensities and systematic absences. The diagram does not predict a complete diffraction pattern or identify a phase. Higher-order diffraction is often represented by different Miller indices with n = 1.

Preset wavelengths are rounded educational values. No wavelength uncertainty, refraction correction, or instrumental calibration is applied.
