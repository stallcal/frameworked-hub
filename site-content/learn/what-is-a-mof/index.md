## Start with a material
MOF-5 offers a concrete starting point: zinc-containing clusters are joined by organic linkers into an extended framework. Its original report connects the structural picture to experiments on porosity. {{< cite "1" >}}

Rather than trying to learn every acronym at once, use one material to ask three questions: What are its building blocks? How do they connect? What evidence tells us about the resulting material?

## Look for the building blocks
{{< editor-note >}}
Develop this section with an annotated figure identifying a metal-containing unit and an organic linker. Explain unfamiliar terms when they first appear. Link each material-specific claim to the relevant reference.
{{< /editor-note >}}

## From chemistry to topology
{{< editor-note >}}
Introduce the idea of describing connections with nodes and links. Show the chemical structure alongside an explicitly labeled simplified net, and explain what information the simplification leaves out. Verify the chosen net assignment against a source before publication.
{{< /editor-note >}}

## Try the reading habit
Before reading a figure caption, write down what you think the picture tells you. Then compare your interpretation with the caption and the experimental discussion.

{{< editor-note >}}
Add a short self-check with an explanation, not just a right answer. This can become an interactive learning moment in a later version.
