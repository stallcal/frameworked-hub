## Three ideas in one name
A metal–organic framework is a coordination network containing organic ligands and potential voids. The IUPAC terminology places MOFs within coordination networks, which in turn belong to the broader family of coordination polymers. {{< cite "1" >}}

The useful words here are **network**, **organic**, and **potential**. They describe an extended arrangement of connected components, its organic ligands, and the possibility of space within that arrangement.

## Potential space is not a measurement
The definition does not require an experimental demonstration of porosity. Guests may occupy the voids, and a framework can change with external conditions. Calling something a MOF therefore does not, by itself, establish how much gas it can adsorb. {{< cite "1" >}}

When reading a paper, keep two questions separate: *What is the material's structure?* and *What properties were measured?*

## A quick self-check
**A structure drawing shows open space. Does that establish a gas-storage capacity?**

No. The drawing helps describe the structure; a capacity claim needs experimental evidence under stated conditions. The name is a starting point for asking better questions, rather than a performance guarantee.
